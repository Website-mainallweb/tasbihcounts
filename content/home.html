What is a tasbih counter?

A tasbih counter is a tool for keeping count while you repeat a remembrance of Allah. The traditional form is a string of prayer beads, called a misbaha or a tasbeeh, usually with thirty-three or ninety-nine beads. A digital tasbih counter does the same job on a screen: you tap once for each repetition and the number rises, so your attention can stay on the words rather than on the arithmetic.

This page is an online tasbih counter you can use straight away. Some people search for it as a tasbeeh counter, some as a dhikr counter or a zikr counter, and in Turkey the same tool is usually called a zikirmatik. In Indonesia it is a tasbih digital or a penghitung tasbih, in France a compteur tasbih. They all describe one thing: a counter that keeps your place while you remember Allah.

Counting on the fingers is the way taught in the sunnah, and a counter is a convenience, not a replacement for it. Use whichever helps you stay consistent.

Targets, rounds and what the numbers mean

A target is the number you are working towards in this sitting. A round is a cycle inside that target: with a round size of one hundred and a goal of one thousand you will complete ten rounds. The two are separate on purpose, and rounds never switch themselves on just because the target is large.

Two kinds of target appear in this app and the difference is deliberate. A target marked Source available has reviewed evidence behind it, such as the thirty-three, thirty-three and thirty-four counted after the obligatory prayer. A target marked Your goal is simply a number you have chosen. Numbers like 313 or 1000 are offered as personal goals and nothing more.

This app will never tell you that a particular repetition count produces a particular result. If you want to count 4444 or 125,000 of something, type it in and the counter will track it faithfully, without attaching a claim to it.

Tasbih, tasbeeh, misbaha and dhikr: what is the difference?

The words overlap and the spellings vary by region, which is why a search for a tasbeeh counter and a search for a dhikr counter often mean the same thing.

Tasbih usually means the act of glorifying Allah, and by extension the beads used to count it. Tasbeeh is the same word written a different way. Misbaha, sibha or sebha is the Arabic name for the bead string itself. Dhikr, also written zikr or zikir, is the wider act of remembrance, of which tasbih is one part. Tahlil means saying La ilaha illallah, tahmid means saying Alhamdulillah, takbir means saying Allahu Akbar and istighfar means seeking forgiveness.

A physical misbaha has no batteries and needs no screen, and many people prefer it. A digital tasbih counter is easier to keep in a pocket, remembers your total between sittings and can hold several dhikr at once. Neither is more correct than the other.

Tips for daily dhikr

A small amount kept up consistently is worth more than a large amount abandoned after a week. Choose a target you can complete on a bad day, not on your best one.

Attach the count to something you already do. The remembrance after salah is the natural anchor, which is why it is the most used routine here. If you count while walking or in the car, turn on vibration and stop looking at the screen; the haptic is designed to be felt rather than watched.
